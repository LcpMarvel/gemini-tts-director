The neighbours

Matter-of-fact and conversational; let the mistaken arithmetic pass without signalling a joke.

The farm next to ours is owned by Mr and Mrs Gregg. The Greggs have two children, both of them boys. Their names are Philip and William. Sometimes I go over to their farm to play with them. I am a girl and I am eight years old. Philip is also eight years old. William is three years older. He is ten.

A small correction

A quick, surprised question; then concede lightly and let the invitation to listen brighten.

What?short pause Oh, all right, then. He is eleven. Last week, something very funny happened to the Gregg family. I am going to tell you about it as best I can.

What she cannot stand

Plainspoken conviction; frustration grows without turning into a lecture.

Now the one thing that Mr Gregg and his two boys loved to do more than anything else was to go hunting. Every Saturday morning they would take their guns and go off into the woods to look for animals and birds to shoot. Even Philip, who was only eight years old, had a gun of his own. I can’t stand hunting. I just can’t stand it. It doesn’t seem right to me that men and boys should kill animals just for the fun they get out of it. So I used to try to stop Philip and William from doing it. Every time I went over to their farm I would do my best to talk them out of it, but they only laughed at me. I even said something about it once to Mr Gregg, but he just walked on past me as if I weren’t there.

The deer

Then, one Saturday morning, I saw Philip and William coming out of the woods with their father, and they were carrying a lovely young deer. This made me so cross that I started shouting at them. The boys laughed and made faces at me, and Mr Gregg told me to go home and mind my own P’s and Q’s.

Losing control

Well, that did it! I saw red. And before I was able to stop myself, I did something I never meant to do. breathI PUT THE MAGIC FINGER ON THEM ALL!

Too late

The anger drains into dismay; the repeated opening comes quickly, then finish more quietly.

Oh, dear! Oh, dear! I even put it on Mrs Gregg, who wasn’t there. I put it on the whole Gregg family.

Remembering Mrs Winter

A rueful aside; ease into the memory without making the regret sentimental.

For months I had been telling myself that I would never put the Magic Finger upon anyone again – not after what happened to my teacher, old Mrs Winter. Poor old Mrs Winter.

Whiskers and a tail

Girl and narration: Leda · Mrs Winter’s question: Kore. V2 · Two-speaker recording completed locally; audio not bundled.

Build suspense, then delighted disbelief; sharpen the teacher’s question and let the final No land matter-of-factly.

Then I got cross, and I saw red, and I put the Magic Finger on Mrs Winter good and strong, and almost at once… Guess what? Whiskers began growing out of her face! They were long black whiskers, just like the ones you see on a cat, only much bigger. And how fast they grew! Before we had time to think, they were out to her ears! Of course the whole class started screaming with laughter, and then Mrs Winter said, ‘Will you be so kind as to tell me what you find so madly funny, all of you?’ And when she turned around to write something on the blackboard we saw that she had grown a tail as well! It was a huge bushy tail! I cannot begin to tell you what happened after that, but if any of you are wondering whether Mrs Winter is quite all right again now, the answer is No. And she never will be.

How the magic feels

Slow down into the physical sensations; gather intensity toward the flash, then leave the final warning hanging.

The Magic Finger is something I have been able to do all my life. I can’t tell you just how I do it, because I don’t even know myself. But it always happens when I get cross, when I see red… Then I get very, very hot all over… Then the tip of the forefinger of my right hand begins to tingle most terribly… And suddenly a sort of flash comes out of me, a quick flash, like something electric. It jumps out and touches the person who has made me cross… And after that the Magic Finger is upon him or her, and things begin to happen…

Waiting for the result

Return to the present story; a quiet decision, then quicken on They happened fast.

Well, the Magic Finger was now upon the whole of the Gregg family, and there was no taking it off again. I ran home and waited for things to happen. They happened fast. I shall now tell you what those things were. I got the whole story from Philip and William the next morning, after it was all over.

Back to the hunt

Keep the counting brisk and plain; briefly brighten Mr Gregg’s quoted boast, then return to the narrator’s distance.

In the afternoon of the very same day that I put the Magic Finger on the Gregg family, Mr Gregg and Philip and William went out hunting once again. This time they were going after wild ducks, so they headed towards the lake. In the first hour they got ten birds. In the next hour they got another six. ‘What a day!’ cried Mr Gregg. ‘This is the best yet!’ He was beside himself with joy.

Four ducks, four shots

Narrow the focus as the ducks approach; speak the four BANGs as four crisp, evenly spaced words, then lower the voice for went the guns.

Just then four more wild ducks flew over their heads. They were flying very low. They were easy to hit. BANG! BANG! BANG! BANG! went the guns.
